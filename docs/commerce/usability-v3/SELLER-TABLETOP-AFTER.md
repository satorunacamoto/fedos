# Seller Tabletop — "Sell a digital product to Alice" (After)

**Date:** 2026-08-13
**Persona:** Bob, a first-time, non-technical seller setting up a small digital-goods shop
**Build under test:** worktree `commerce-purchase-integration`, CandidateHead commit `d953c37bf1dfd4cc018ffa7601d974b09fc25319`
**Method:** Real Electron app instance (`electron dist/fedos_electron_beta_main.cjs --remote-debugging-port=9223 --user-data-dir=...tabletop-seller-userdata`), driven live over Chrome DevTools Protocol with a hand-written Node.js `ws`-free script (native `WebSocket`/`fetch`) calling `Runtime.evaluate` and `Page.captureScreenshot`. No test harness, no source reading beyond `package.json` for launch commands, no shortcuts — every click/fill/select below was a real DOM interaction against the running renderer, verified against real screenshots. 37 screenshots saved to a local scratchpad (not in the repo).

## 1. Narrative walkthrough

**Launch.** First screen: *"Welcome. No local profile was found on this device."* plus a stark disclaimer — *"This is a Closed Beta build: no independent, external security audit has been performed, and the installer is not code-signed. Do not use it to protect information where compromise would cause serious harm."* Only one button: **Create a profile**. Blunt and a little alarming for a completely fresh user, but honest — I appreciated that it didn't pretend to be more finished than it is.

**Profile creation.** Clicking through led to a form: Profile label, Passphrase, Confirm passphrase, with a serious warning that the passphrase is never stored and there is no password reset. I typed a label ("Bob's Digital Shop") and a passphrase, submitted, and landed straight into the main app shell: *"Profile unlocked — Runtime ready."* No email, no cloud account, no waiting — reassuring for a privacy-first tool, though it also means there is no safety net if I forget the passphrase (which the app told me up front, so no complaint there).

**Finding "My Store."** The top nav has Conversations, **Shop**, **My Store**, Search, Archive, Devices, Safety Center, Settings — plus a persistent "No Relay configured" notice in the corner. As a seller, "My Store" was the obvious click.

**The "Acting as" surprise.** My Store opened with a badge: *"Fictional/local demo data only. LOCAL DEMO,"* and a dropdown labeled **Acting as**, defaulted to **"Customer — Alice (demo)."** I had just clicked into *my own* store and was immediately looking at it as a fake customer, not as myself — with the page telling me *"Demo fixture identity — not your real profile, not a real merchant/customer."* This was genuinely confusing for a first click: I expected "My Store" to default to me. The dropdown also offered "My Store — Bob's Digital Shop (real profile)," which is what I actually wanted, so I switched to it manually.

**Creating a product.** The **Create Product** form has exactly four fields: Title, Description, Price, Currency. I filled in "FedOS Starter Wallpaper Pack," a description mentioning "10 desktop wallpapers (PNG, 4K), instant download after purchase," and $4.99. There is **no field anywhere in this form for attaching the actual digital file** — no upload box, no "add content," nothing. I clicked **Create Product (draft)** anyway, since the app gave me nothing else to do.

**"Attach Image" is a red herring for delivery.** Once the draft existed, a file picker appeared next to an **Attach Image** button. I inspected it — `accept="image/png,image/jpeg"` — it is a product-photo uploader, not a deliverable-content uploader. I never found any control, anywhere in the product or order lifecycle (draft → active → order created → submitted → accepted → paid → fulfilling → delivered → completed), that lets a seller attach the actual thing a buyer is paying for. I did not attach an image (not required) and clicked **Publish**. The product flipped to "active."

**A trust problem: my own listing showed "Unknown merchant."** Switching to the Shop tab (still viewable while "Acting as" me), my own live listing appeared as *"FedOS Starter Wallpaper Pack — 4.99 USD — Unknown merchant."* Every other demo listing correctly showed its shop name ("Northlight Studio," "Riverbend Makerspace"). My own real listing did not show "Bob's Digital Shop." This is a real bug, not a demo-data quirk — it's the same product I just created as my real profile.

**Playing Alice (per the task's fallback instruction).** There was no separate live buyer to sell to in this window, so — as instructed — I switched "Acting as" to **"Customer — Alice (demo)."** As Alice, I opened the product ("Sold by Unknown merchant" — the bug follows through to the buyer's product page too), and used the **Create Order** section (Quantity defaulted to 1) to place an order, then clicked **Submit Order**. Order status: `submitted`. The seller field on the order screen displayed a raw pubkey (`6651bb5ff290…`) instead of "Bob's Digital Shop" — the same attribution bug again.

**Switching back to Bob.** "My Store" now showed a clear, reassuring callout: *"Needs your attention — 1 New order — awaiting your Accept/Reject."* The badge count on the "My Store" tab itself also went from 0 to 1. I opened the order and clicked **Accept**.

**Paying as Alice.** Back as Alice, the order now read *"Merchant accepted — pay now, then (local demo only) simulate an authoritative payment confirmation."* This line made it explicit that payment here is a simulation, not a real transaction — appreciated honesty, but it means the entire "sale" I completed is not evidence of a working real-money or real-network flow. I clicked **Pay Now**, which generated a `local-sim-payment-…` reference, then clicked a clearly labeled **Simulate Payment (TEST/LOCAL) — NOT REAL MONEY** button. Status became `paid`.

**The delivery moment — and the gap.** The paid-order screen showed a new section: *"Digital content from this purchase, if the seller attached any"* with an **Open Purchased Content** button. As Alice, I clicked it. Result: **"this product has no protected content."** This is the moment the task's core premise broke down — I had "sold a digital product," collected simulated payment, and the buyer's own purchased-content viewer confirmed there was nothing to open, because the seller flow never gave me anywhere to attach it.

**Fulfillment is a status label, not a delivery action.** Back as Bob, "My Store" flagged *"1 Paid — ready to fulfill."* Opening the order gave exactly one button: **Mark Fulfilling**. No attach-file step appeared here either. I clicked it; status became `fulfilling`.

**Buyer confirms receipt of nothing.** As Alice again, the order read: *"Merchant has fulfilled this order — once you receive it, confirm receipt yourself. Only your own confirmation (never a merchant claim alone) can mark this delivered."* This is well-intentioned, anti-tampering copy — but it does not warn the buyer that "Open Purchased Content" already told her there was no content. A real, less careful buyer could easily click **I Received This** without ever having received anything, exactly as I did to move the task forward. Status became `delivered`, then I clicked **Complete Order** and status became `completed`. The app added: *"Order completed. Reputation records were saved for both parties."*

**Signed receipt.** As a final nicety, Bob's order screen offered **Issue Signed Receipt**. My first click silently failed (a stale-DOM click that didn't register — recovered by reopening the order and clicking again). The second attempt worked: a QR code appeared with **"Verification status: VALID"** and the underlying signed-receipt text. A nice, reassuring proof-of-sale artifact.

**Confirming from Bob's side.** Switching back to "Acting as" Bob, "My Store" showed the product still "active," the order listed as **"Customer — Alice (demo) — completed,"** and **"My Reputation"** now read *"Persona a18cf48c9879… — 1-4 verified trades — mostly other — persona epoch currently valid."* — up from "No reputation history on record" at the start.

## 2. Was the task completed, and how do I know?

**Yes, in the sense the app's own state machine defines "completed."** The order's `Status:` field read **`completed`** on screen, both when viewed as buyer (Alice) and as seller (Bob), independently. Bob's product list showed the order as "completed." Bob's reputation record changed from "no history" to "1-4 verified trades." A cryptographically signed, self-verified ("VALID") QR receipt was issued.

**No, in the sense the task's literal premise ("sell a *digital* product... complete the transaction") was not actually fulfilled end-to-end.** The buyer's own "Open Purchased Content" screen stated plainly: *"this product has no protected content."* Alice paid, and received nothing, because the seller-side flow never offered a way to attach anything a buyer could receive. The order-completion machinery (accept → pay → fulfill → confirm receipt → complete) worked flawlessly as a *status* pipeline, but the actual "content to deliver" that defines a *digital* product was never technically possible to attach — not a mistake I made, a capability gap in the product-creation and order-fulfillment forms.

## 3. Restart / follow-up verification checks

1. **Does the listing/order survive an app restart?** Killed the tracked Electron process (PID confirmed via `Get-CimInstance`, zero orphan `electron.exe` from my session afterward), relaunched against the identical `--user-data-dir`. Result: **YES.** The app came up locked (*"This device has a locked profile... Profile: Bob's Digital Shop"*), and after re-entering the passphrase, "My Store" (once switched to "Acting as" Bob) showed the product still "active" at 4.99 USD, the order still listed as "completed," and the reputation record ("Persona a18cf48c9879…, 1-4 verified trades") intact and unchanged. Full persistence confirmed.

2. **Does "Acting as" remember your last selection across a reload?** **NO.** After restart, "My Store" again defaulted to "Customer — Alice (demo)" and showed "No products yet" until I manually reselected "My Store — Bob's Digital Shop (real profile)." This is the same disorienting default a brand-new seller hits on their very first visit — it also happens every time the app restarts, not just once.

3. **Can the seller tell whether the buyer actually received/opened anything?** **NO — and this is close to a design gap, not just a missing feature.** Bob's own order-detail view (as merchant) shows only a status label ("Delivery status: completed"); there is no "Open Purchased Content" control on the seller's side at all (that only exists in the buyer's view), and nothing indicates whether any file was opened, downloaded, or even existed. The entire signal Bob gets is Alice's one-sided, un-auditable self-attestation ("I Received This"), which the UI itself even documents as intentional — "Only your own confirmation... can mark this delivered" — but that same design means a seller has *zero* independent evidence a real delivery occurred, and (as observed directly) that self-attestation can legitimately fire even when the product had no content to deliver in the first place.

## 4. Friction points

- **P0 — No way to attach actual digital deliverable content, anywhere in the flow.** The product-creation form has only Title/Description/Price/Currency. The post-creation "Attach Image" control is restricted to `image/png,image/jpeg` and is a thumbnail/photo field, not a deliverable. No attach-content step exists at any order stage (paid, fulfilling). The buyer's own "Open Purchased Content" screen confirms: *"this product has no protected content."* A seller genuinely cannot sell a digital product with this build — only list a *description* of one.
- **P0 — Buyers can confirm "I Received This" for a product with zero attached content, with no warning.** The buyer-side copy encourages self-attested receipt confirmation as the trust anchor of the whole order-completion flow, but nothing blocks or warns when there is nothing to receive. This closes the order, credits seller reputation, and issues a "VALID" signed receipt for a transaction where no digital good ever changed hands — a real risk surface for both accidental and deliberate buyer/seller error.
- **P1 — Seller's own real listing is attributed to "Unknown merchant"** in the Shop view, on the product detail page ("Sold by Unknown merchant"), and on the order screen (raw pubkey shown instead of "Bob's Digital Shop"). Every demo listing shows a proper shop name; only the real, just-created listing is broken. Undermines buyer trust and seller confidence simultaneously.
- **P1 — "Acting as" defaults to a demo customer identity, not the seller's own real profile,** every time "My Store" or "Shop" is opened (including after every app restart). A first-time seller clicking into "My Store" is shown someone else's (fake) view first and must notice and manually switch — easy to miss, and repeats forever.
- **P1 — The entire guided seller/buyer flow is explicitly local-only simulation** ("LOCAL DEMO," "Fictional/local demo data only," "(local demo only) simulate an authoritative payment confirmation," "NOT REAL MONEY"), and "No Relay configured" / "Network sync is off for this profile" is shown on nearly every screen throughout. Nothing in this run demonstrates the product would actually become visible to, or purchasable by, a real independent buyer on another machine — onboarding never prompted me to configure a Relay even while actively publishing a "live" listing.
- **P2 — "Issue Signed Receipt" silently failed to register on the first click** (no error shown, button just did nothing); reopening the order and clicking again worked. Minor, but a first-time user gets zero feedback on the failed click.
- **P2 — Stale caption text observed once:** immediately after switching "Acting as" to "My Store — Bob's Digital Shop (real profile)" for the first time, the helper line below still read *"Demo fixture identity — not your real profile, not a real merchant/customer"* (contradicting the selection). Later switches back to Bob correctly showed *"Your real local profile, acting as this store's merchant."* Possibly a one-off render-lag rather than a systemic bug, but worth a look.

## 5. Score

**Score: 3/10.**

Justification: the order-status pipeline itself (draft → active → order → submit → accept → pay → fulfill → deliver → complete), restart persistence, reputation recording, and the signed-receipt/QR mechanism all worked cleanly and were genuinely reassuring in their wording and status transitions — that's real, working infrastructure. But the task was literally "sell this digital product... and complete the transaction," and the app has **no mechanism to attach the digital content being sold**, which the buyer's own screen confirms outright ("this product has no protected content"). A seller can walk through every step, get a green "completed" status, a signed receipt, and improved reputation — while having delivered nothing. That is not a rough edge on an otherwise-working digital-commerce feature; it is the feature's central promise not being deliverable at all in this build, compounded by a merchant-attribution bug ("Unknown merchant") on the one real listing that existed. I am not scoring based on effort or the polish of the status-machine copy — I am scoring based on whether Bob could actually sell Alice a digital product, and he could not.

**SELLER ZERO-INSTRUCTION: YES WITH FRICTION** — every screen I needed was discoverable by clicking around and reading the on-screen copy (which is unusually well-written and self-explanatory for a beta), and I never got stuck on *navigation*. But a real first-time seller would very likely land on "My Store" seeing someone else's fake data by default, would look in vain for any way to attach their actual product file, and would see their own listing mislabeled "Unknown merchant" — three distinct, avoidable points of doubt that a careful user would notice and could not resolve without outside help (there is no in-app hint pointing at "Acting as" being wrong-by-default, and no acknowledgment anywhere that content-attachment is simply not supported yet).

**SELLER ATTENTION sub-score: 7/10.** Once you are looking at "My Store" as your real (correctly-selected) merchant identity, the attention signaling is genuinely good: a dedicated badge count on the "My Store" tab itself, a "Needs your attention" callout that names the exact required action in plain language ("New order — awaiting your Accept/Reject," then later "Paid — ready to fulfill"), and per-order status lines throughout. Points lost because (a) this whole signal is invisible by default — the tab opens on a demo customer's empty view, not the seller's, so a distracted seller might not even reach the screen where "needs attention" lives, and (b) nothing anywhere flags the deeper problem that an order can reach "completed" with reputation awarded despite zero content ever being delivered — the UI is attentive about status transitions but blind to the substance of what was (or wasn't) actually delivered.
