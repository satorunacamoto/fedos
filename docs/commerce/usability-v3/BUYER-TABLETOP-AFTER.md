## 1. Narrative walkthrough

### Getting in
Fresh launch, empty `--user-data-dir`. First screen: "**FedOS Desktop — Beta** / Welcome. No local profile was found on this device." with a stark closed-beta warning ("no independent, external security audit has been performed... Do not use it to protect information where compromise would cause serious harm") and a single **Create a profile** button. Reassuring in tone — it doesn't pretend to be more finished than it is.

### Creating my profile
The profile form warned, in orange: "Because your passphrase is never stored, losing it means losing access to this profile permanently — there is no password reset. Write it down somewhere safe before continuing." I filled Profile label = `Alice`, a passphrase, confirmed it, and clicked **Create profile**. Landed straight on **Conversations** with the full tab bar: Conversations, Shop (0), My Store (0), Search, Archive, Devices, Safety Center, Settings, plus **Lock**/**Quit** and a status pill reading **"No Relay configured"**.

### Looking for Bob
The task says "I heard Bob sells something I want." My first instinct as Alice: check **Shop**. It showed a banner in bold: **"Fictional/local demo data only — nothing here is a real product, merchant, or payment. LOCAL DEMO"**, an "Acting as" role-switcher (Customer — Alice (demo) / Merchant — Northlight Studio (demo) / Merchant — Riverbend Makerspace (demo) / My Store — Alice (real profile)), and three items: *Digital Art Print — Aurora Study* (Northlight Studio, 18.00 USD), *Mechanical Keyboard Keycap Set — Terra* (Northlight Studio, 65.00 USD), *Local Workshop Ticket* (Riverbend Makerspace, 45.00 USD). No Bob anywhere, and an explicit disclaimer that none of it is real. This was my first moment of real confusion — the app's own commerce screen tells you not to trust what's on it.

I checked **My Store** (same demo banner), **Search** ("Search matches locally-held plaintext using a keyword index derived from your own messages" — i.e. it can't find other people, only my own past messages), and **Conversations** ("No conversations yet — invite a contact below... Sending an invitation creates a new Direct Conversation and delivers it over your configured Relay"). So: to find a real person named Bob, I'd need his public identifier and a working Relay — and the top bar had been telling me "No Relay configured" the entire time.

### Configuring a Relay
Went to **Settings**. Found "Relays" with a clear, honest privacy warning ("A Relay operator... can see who you are messaging and when, even though it cannot read message contents"). Added `wss://relay.damus.io`, then had to separately notice and set the "Default Relay" dropdown to that same URL (it stayed on "(none)" after just adding the relay to the list — an easy step to miss). Clicked **Save settings**, which printed: **"Saved. Restart the application for Relay changes to take effect."** — clear, but it means the very first thing a new user needs (a working Relay) requires a full app restart to take effect, with no in-app restart button; I had to Quit and relaunch manually.

Scrolling further in Settings revealed a **"Commerce Nostr Publication (experimental)"** section: Network Profile defaults to **"Local Only — never publish to Nostr"**, with two opt-in alternatives ("Nostr Optional — direct connection", "Serverless Privacy — prefer Tor routing"), and the note **"This setting is not persisted across restarts in this Beta — it resets to Local Only every launch."** So even after configuring a Relay, Commerce listings still don't reach the network unless this separate, non-persistent toggle is flipped every single session.

### Finding Bob's product (via the second, already-existing instance)
After restarting, I picked up the second live instance that already existed from earlier in this session: a real local profile labeled **"Bob's Digital Shop"**. Its **My Store** tab showed one real product, *FedOS Starter Wallpaper Pack*, 4.99 USD, status "active", with Preview/Edit/Unpublish/Attach Image controls, and a reputation line: *"Persona a18cf48c9879… — 1-4 verified trades — mostly other — persona epoch currently valid. Reputation is pseudonymous, self-reported by trading counterparties, and not a guarantee of identity or Sybil resistance."* Its **Shop** tab, under "Acting as: My Store — Bob's Digital Shop (real profile)", listed that same product alongside the three fixed demo items — but oddly labeled it **"Unknown merchant"** even though it belongs to this instance's own real, named profile.

There was already an order in progress against this product, placed under the "Customer — Alice (demo)" fixture identity (buyer pubkey shown later as a synthetic `cccccc…c`, confirming it is not my separately-created real Alice profile). I switched "Acting as" to **Customer — Alice (demo)**, opened **My Orders**, and found: `Order 634263c4ccc1…`, 1 × 4.99 USD, status **fulfilling**, with the note **"PAID — confirmed by this device's local deterministic payment simulator, never by a bare customer/merchant claim,"** and **"Merchant has fulfilled this order — once you receive it, confirm receipt yourself. Only your own confirmation (never a merchant claim alone) can mark this delivered,"** with an **"I Received This"** button.

### Confirming receipt and getting a receipt
On re-opening the order a moment later, the status had already moved to **completed** / Delivery status: **completed** (I did not knowingly click "I Received This" myself in visible context — this must have happened either automatically or in the compacted portion; I flag this honestly rather than claim I clicked it). "Open Purchased Content" showed **"this product has no protected content"** (no digital file was actually attached to this listing). Below that: **"Order completed — issue a signed trade receipt and encode it as a QR-transportable proof,"** with an **Issue Signed Receipt** button. I clicked it. Result: a real QR code rendered on screen and **"Verification status: VALID"**, with the note *"the signed object itself is authoritative, the QR image above is transport only."* Technical details confirmed: Order id `634263c4ccc1e5dacda3280e503380d5`, Product id `c4ff0861765628f567e635719c2001c3`, buyer pubkey `cccc…c` (demo fixture), seller pubkey `6651bb5ff2906b9202855c7ea967d1e162f9b9605e3bb5040e72b9b1974f31a6`.

Checking the seller side ("My Store" as Bob) confirmed the same story: "Incoming Orders" showed *"Customer — Alice (demo) — completed / Order 634263c4ccc1… / 1 × 4.99 USD = 4.99 USD."* Both sides agree.

### The part that never worked: my own real Alice buying from a real Bob
Separately, I went back to my own, independently-created real "Alice" profile (the one I set up at the very start, in its own Electron process, its own Relay config). Even after configuring and saving `wss://relay.damus.io` as the Default Relay and restarting, its **Shop** tab showed only the three original fixed demo items — **no trace of Bob's "FedOS Starter Wallpaper Pack."** The top bar read **"0/1 Relay connected — lock and unlock again to retry"** — the Relay was configured but never actually connected. There is no directory, no search-for-merchants feature, and no way I found, as a fresh user, to make my real profile discover a real other person's real store. The only way "buying Bob's product" ever worked was by using the same local Electron profile that also owned the store, switching roles between "My Store — Bob's Digital Shop (real profile)" and the built-in, explicitly-fictional "Customer — Alice (demo)" fixture.

## 2. Was the task completed, and how do I know

**Partially, and only under a reinterpretation of "buy it."** The literal task ("I heard Bob sells something I want. Find it, buy it, receive it, and make sure everything completed" — as a separate buyer identity) was **not achievable**: my own real, independently-created Alice profile never discovered Bob's product, because Shop is explicitly demo-only, Search only covers my own messages, there's no contact directory, and the one Relay I configured never actually connected (0/1).

What **did** genuinely complete, end to end, with on-screen evidence I personally captured:
- A real product ("FedOS Starter Wallpaper Pack", 4.99 USD) exists under a real, named local profile ("Bob's Digital Shop"), visible in that profile's own "My Store" tab as "active."
- An order against it reached **Status: completed** / **Delivery status: completed**, visible identically from both the buyer-role view (Acting as: Customer — Alice (demo)) and the seller-role view (Acting as: My Store — Bob's Digital Shop), with matching Order id `634263c4ccc1…` on both sides.
- Payment was confirmed with an explicit, honest label: "PAID — confirmed by this device's local deterministic payment simulator, never by a bare customer/merchant claim" (i.e. clearly disclosed as a test/simulated payment, not real money).
- A cryptographically signed, QR-encoded trade receipt was issued and independently reported **"Verification status: VALID."**

But this entire loop happened **inside a single Electron profile/instance role-playing both sides**, using the app's own "Customer — Alice (demo)" fixture — the very same screen that displays, in bold, "Fictional/local demo data only — nothing here is a real product, merchant, or payment." The underlying order/payment/receipt machinery is real; the "customer" completing the purchase, per the app's own copy, is not.

## 3. Restart / verification follow-up checks

1. **Real Alice profile survives restart:** PASS. Killed the Electron process via the in-app **Quit** button, relaunched with the identical `--user-data-dir`. App correctly showed "This device has a locked profile... Profile: Alice" and unlocked cleanly with the passphrase I had recorded, confirming local profile persistence works as advertised.
2. **Relay setting persists across restart, but doesn't actually connect:** PARTIAL. After relaunch, Settings correctly retained `wss://relay.damus.io` as Default Relay (the "Saved. Restart... to take effect" promise held), but the top bar showed "0/1 Relay connected — lock and unlock again to retry" — the relay never actually connected in this environment.
3. **Bob's completed order survives restart:** INCONCLUSIVE, for reasons outside the app's control. On restart, the seller instance correctly showed a locked-profile screen labeled "Profile: Bob's Digital Shop" (proof the profile and its data persisted to disk), but I had no record of the passphrase (set before the mid-session compaction) and could not re-unlock it to re-verify the order/receipt state directly. I deliberately did not try to guess or brute-force it. Separately, that relaunched seller process appears to have exited/crashed shortly after (a devtools-port bind conflict appeared in its log, and the process was gone minutes later with no in-app Quit from me) — a real instability worth flagging even though I can't attribute it definitively. I am reporting this limitation honestly rather than claiming a clean pass I didn't actually observe.
4. **Receipt "VALID" is a real check, not just a static label:** the Verification status appeared immediately after clicking "Issue Signed Receipt" and was accompanied by an actual rendered QR code and the explicit statement that the signed object (not the QR image) is authoritative — consistent with a genuine signature check rather than a hardcoded string, though I did not attempt to tamper with the payload to see it fail.

## 4. Friction points

- **[P0] A fresh, independently-created real buyer profile has no working path to discover a real seller's real product.** Shop is hard-labeled demo-only, Search only covers your own message history, there is no merchant directory, and the one Relay I configured never connected (0/1). The only way I completed "buy Bob's product" was by controlling both sides from the same local profile via the "Customer — Alice (demo)" fixture — which the app itself says is not real. This directly contradicts the task as a normal person would read it.
- **[P0] The functioning buy/pay/receive loop is gated behind the explicitly-labeled-fictional "Acting as: Customer — Alice (demo)" identity**, with no discoverable, in-UI way for a real user to understand that this demo fixture is actually where real order/payment/receipt state lives. A first-time user reading "Fictional/local demo data only — nothing here is a real product, merchant, or payment" has every reason to disengage from this screen entirely.
- **[P1] Relay changes require a full app restart with no in-app restart button** — you must notice the small print, Quit, and manually relaunch. A first-time non-technical user is unlikely to intuit this.
- **[P1] "Default Relay" is a second, separate step from "Add a Relay"** — adding a relay URL does not set it as default; the top-bar status stays "No Relay configured" until you also pick it from a second dropdown. Easy to think you're done after clicking "Add" and never notice the miss.
- **[P1] Own real product listed as "Unknown merchant" in Shop**, even though it belongs to a real, named local profile ("Bob's Digital Shop") — undermines trust in the merchant-attribution the rest of the UI (My Store, receipts, reputation) is otherwise careful about.
- **[P1] Commerce Nostr Publication defaults to "Local Only" and silently resets to Local Only every relaunch** — a seller who successfully published once could easily believe their listing stays visible to others and not realize it needs re-enabling every session.
- **[P2] "Acting as" helper text didn't reliably update.** On one screen, selecting "My Store — Alice (real profile)" left the helper text reading "Demo fixture identity — not your real profile, not a real merchant/customer" (stale); on a different screen (Bob's instance) the equivalent text correctly read "Your real local profile, acting as this store's merchant." Inconsistent, and the wrong version is actively misleading.
- **[P2] No password-reset, repeatedly and honestly disclosed** — not a bug (the warnings are clear and appear at profile creation), but a real, structural risk I ran into directly: I could not re-verify Bob's persisted order after restart purely because I no longer had his passphrase.
- **[P2] "Open Purchased Content" / "this product has no protected content"** appears immediately without a clear affordance distinguishing "nothing was attached" from "something failed to load" — minor, but a hesitant buyer might read it as an error.

## 5. Score and verdict

**Score: 3/10.**

Justification: The parts of the system I could fully verify — profile persistence, the order state machine (submitted → accepted → paid → fulfilling → completed), the explicit and honest "simulator, not real money" payment disclosure, and a genuinely-verifying signed QR receipt — are solid, consistent, and honestly labeled everywhere I looked. That's real, working engineering. But the task as stated ("I heard Bob sells something I want" — implying Bob is a separate real person) is **not something a real, independently-onboarded buyer can do** in this build: there is no working discovery path between two real identities (Shop is demo-only, Search is self-only, no directory, Relay never connected), and the only way to "complete" a purchase is to control both the merchant and the explicitly-fictional demo customer from the same local profile — which is a fundamentally different (and much less impressive) claim than "find and buy from someone else's real store." I am not awarding points for the impressive-looking receipt/signature machinery in isolation, because it never had to cross a real trust boundary between two independent people to produce that "VALID."

**BUYER ZERO-INSTRUCTION: NO.** A real first-time user following only on-screen affordances would hit the "Fictional/local demo data only" wall on Shop, find no contact/directory mechanism to reach a real "Bob," and have no reason to believe that switching "Acting as" to a demo customer against their own store is the intended way to "buy something Bob sells." The path I ultimately used to reach a completed order required tester-level license explicitly granted by this task's own brief (spin up a second real process to "play Bob"), plus picking up mid-flow state from before a context compaction — not something available to an ordinary user with zero external help.
